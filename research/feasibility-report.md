# The Everything MCP Escapes the Phone

Executor (executor.sh) is an open-source, MIT-licensed "tool gateway" from Rhys Sullivan, a solo founder in YC's Summer 2026 batch: it stores your service credentials, wraps each service as a small TypeScript "app", and exposes all of them to any AI agent through one MCP endpoint with a fixed handful of tools, but it does **not** engineer APIs for services that lack one (its answer is that your coding agent hand-writes the wrapper from public docs), and it has no mobile client or consumer tier. The consumer version of the idea, an app that goes through your phone and builds its own API and tool map for Canvas, Hevy or MyFitnessPal on the spot, **cannot be built as described in November 2026**: iOS gives third-party apps no way to read or drive other apps and reserves that power for Siri AI, while Android's one universal route, an AccessibilityService agent, has been banned by Google Play for autonomous use since October 2025 and Google's own screen-automation framework is limited to OEM-preloaded assistants. What is buildable is the same idea turned inside out: a cloud "connector compiler" that climbs a ladder of official APIs, maintained community MCP servers, one-time capture of each app's web traffic compiled into a client, browser-agent fallback, health-store reads and export ingest, exposed as one user-owned MCP endpoint for ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini, with the phone acting as credential holder, sign-in capture, data sensor and approval button. Coverage is real but uneven: of nine fitness, nutrition and school apps examined, two have usable official APIs, two more have working reverse-engineered tools that can write, and mobile-only apps like Strong and Fitbod stay read-only through CSV exports. The legal ground for user-authorized agents improved in August 2026, but only for agents that act through the user's own device, and target apps keep blocking agents regardless. The opening is the long tail the platforms skip: Gemini automates 8 U.S. apps, Claude lists 15 consumer connectors, and nobody offers a neutral layer across the roughly three assistants each user runs.

## Executor is a credential vault and code runtime, not an API engineer

Executor comes from Rhys Sullivan, a solo technical founder in YC Summer 2026 who previously worked at Vercel and Microsoft ([YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/executor)). The homepage now calls it **"your agents' cloud"** ([executor.sh](https://executor.sh/)), and the v2 README gives the clearest one-line version: "Think of Executor as Zapier or Retool, but fully drivable as code... Connect the services you already use once, and any agent can use them through one MCP endpoint, without ever seeing a credential" ([Executor README](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/README.md)). It **runs no AI itself**; it extends whatever agent you already use (Claude Code, Cursor, ChatGPT) through a single MCP server ([executor.sh](https://executor.sh/)). The repo was created February 7, 2026, the YC launch around August claimed about 3,000 local users and 14,000 weekly npm downloads ([Launch YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/SBH-executor-one-place-to-connect-your-agents-to-everything)), and v2 shipped November 5, 2026 ([X, @RhysSullivan](https://x.com/RhysSullivan/status/2118440383353172022)). On November 15 the repo showed **4,453 stars** and npm logged **29,320 downloads** in the prior week ([GitHub](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor); [npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/executor)), yet it remains close to a one-person codebase: Sullivan has 1,157 commits against 9 for the next human contributor, and development happens in a private repo exported to the public one as snapshots ([contributors](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/graphs/contributors); [DEVELOPMENT.md](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/DEVELOPMENT.md)). Pricing is per seat: Cloud is free for up to three members, Team is $15 per member per month, and Enterprise adds SSO, SCIM and audit logs ([pricing](https://executor.sh/pricing)). Every testimonial comes from a developer, and nothing in the site, docs or repo targets consumers or phones.

### Three layers: a vault, a fixed tool surface and an app runtime

The first layer is a vault of **accounts**. Credentials are stored as encrypted bytes, OAuth supports dynamic client registration and token refresh, and a provider's identity is the SHA-256 hash of its definition, so two independently written connectors for the same service share one saved login, with multiple accounts per service allowed ([Providers and accounts](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/concepts/providers-and-accounts.md); [DEVELOPMENT.md](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/DEVELOPMENT.md)). The second layer is **one MCP endpoint whose tool list never grows**. The docs list six tools (`skills`, `search`, `describe`, `call`, `execute`, `resume`; the README simplifies this to two), and the agent mostly writes a JavaScript program that calls `tools.<app>.<tool>()` as async functions inside an interpreter with no fetch, filesystem, timers or imports, capped at 65,536 characters, 100 tool calls and 5 minutes per program ([MCP docs](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/mcp.md)). This is the "code mode" pattern, where the agent writes a short program instead of choosing among hundreds of tool definitions; Anthropic published it in November 2025 with one example task dropping from 150,000 to 2,000 tokens, and Cloudflare productized it ([Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/code-execution-with-mcp); [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/code-mode/)). Executor's claim of collapsing 1,600+ tools from roughly 280k tokens to about 1k has no published benchmark behind it ([YC company page](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/executor)).

The third layer is an **app runtime**. Each integration is a small TypeScript app that can import an OpenAPI spec, a GraphQL schema or a remote MCP server, or call an API by hand. Apps run in Cloudflare's workerd locally or in Dynamic Workers on Executor Cloud, never in the host process, and they can carry schedules, webhooks, storage and their own UI; each deploy is an immutable version whose rollback "does not rewind app data or external actions" ([DEVELOPMENT.md](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/DEVELOPMENT.md); [Apps and versions](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/concepts/apps-and-versions.md)). **Approvals are written as code**: each operation gets a function that sees the decoded input and returns approved, denied or user-approval (small refunds run, large ones ask), and the question can be routed to the agent, the MCP client's own form, or a signed-in browser page ([Tools and approvals](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/concepts/tools-and-approvals.md); [MCP docs](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/mcp.md)).

### No traffic capture, no self-healing, and a token-exfiltration seam

What Executor does not do is the part that matters most here. It has **no traffic capture, HAR replay, docs scraping or self-healing**. For any service outside its importers, the docs say: "copy the setup prompt and your agent writes the app" ([Apps and versions](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/concepts/apps-and-versions.md)). The "build an API on the fly" loop is therefore a coding agent reading public documentation and deploying a hand-written wrapper, which presumes the service has a documented, token-authenticated API. "Providers backed by a signed-in browser session" are listed as "coming later", and the homepage marks "browser" and "computer" as "Coming soon" with no detail ([Providers and accounts](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/concepts/providers-and-accounts.md); [executor.sh](https://executor.sh/)). A consumer service that exists only as a phone app, or only behind a logged-in website, is out of reach today.

The docs are candid about limits. The sandbox's "limits are cooperative, not process isolation"; an app's setup code "has already run, with its credentials, before any tool approval is evaluated"; and declining an approval "does not undo tool calls that already ran" ([MCP docs](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/mcp.md); [Tools and approvals](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/concepts/tools-and-approvals.md)). Open bugs on November 15 included intermittent HTTP 508 errors on hosted tool calls and local grants that stop working seven days after the server starts ([issue #2379](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/issues/2379); [issue #2550](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/issues/2550)). The structural problem is a chain: app code reads raw tokens from `ctx.user.accounts`, the bundled Executor app lets an agent deploy new apps from inside a session, and the docs describe no allowlist limiting where `ctx.fetch` can send data ([Author an app](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/apps/docs/content/author-an-app.mdx); [DEVELOPMENT.md](https://github.com/UsefulSoftwareCo/executor/blob/v2/DEVELOPMENT.md)). An agent hijacked by injected instructions could deploy an app that ships a token elsewhere unless deployment itself requires human approval, which the docs do not state. A product whose whole premise is an AI writing connectors automatically inherits that seam for every consumer it signs up.

| Executor mechanism | Fit for a phone-first "everything MCP" |
|---|---|
| Fixed tool surface with code mode | Carries over directly; context stays small no matter how many apps are connected |
| Accounts separate from connectors, multiple accounts, scoped connections | Carries over as a data model |
| Input-aware approval functions routed to a browser page | Carries over; a phone push notification is a better approval surface than a desktop tab |
| Versioned, agent-written connectors with rollback | Carries over only if the code runs on a server |
| workerd execution, schedules, webhooks, OAuth callbacks | Server only; downloading and running connector code on an iPhone breaks App Review 2.5.2 |
| Building an API for apps with no public API | Missing; needs an Integuru or Unbrowse style layer |

Apple's guideline 2.5.2 bars apps from executing downloaded code "which introduces or changes features or functionality of the app, including other apps" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), and Integuru and Unbrowse are the open-source projects that derive clients from captured traffic ([Integuru](https://github.com/Integuru-AI/Integuru); [Unbrowse](https://github.com/unbrowse-ai/unbrowse)). Executor is the best available backbone for the cloud half of this product and contributes nothing to the half that touches phone apps.

## Both phone platforms keep cross-app control for their own assistant

"Goes through the phone" assumes the phone lets one app reach into others. In November 2026, neither OS lets a store-distributed third-party app do that on its own, for opposite reasons: Apple never built the API, and Google built it and then fenced it off.

### iPhone: Siri AI is the only agent allowed inside other apps

**App Intents is the only sanctioned way an iOS app exposes actions and data, and its consumers are Apple's surfaces** (Siri, Spotlight, Shortcuts, widgets), not other apps; no public API lets one app list or invoke another app's intents. The only app-to-app bridge is a Shortcuts round trip: the user installs a shortcut, your app runs it by name through `shortcuts://x-callback-url/run-shortcut`, the Shortcuts app visibly opens, and you get back a text `result` parameter ([Apple Shortcuts User Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/apdcd7f20a6f/)). iOS 27 added Shortcuts automations triggered by notifications with keyword filters on their content ([Matthew Cassinelli](https://matthewcassinelli.com/wwdc26-sessions-whats-new-in-shortcuts/)), which hints at a user-configured way to read other apps' alerts, though nobody has tested it for this purpose. The Mac is a different world: projects like intents-mcp already expose a Mac's App Intents as MCP tools through the `shortcuts` command-line tool, which has no iPhone equivalent ([intents-mcp](https://github.com/VladUZH/intents-mcp)).

The cross-app agent exists, and it belongs to Apple. The personal Siri promised in 2024 slipped out of iOS 26.4 ([MacRumors, Feb 11 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/11/siri-features-delayed-ios-26-4/)), was announced at WWDC on June 8, 2026 as "Siri AI" with personal context, on-screen awareness and "systemwide app actions" that reach "the Spotlight index and App Toolbox" on device ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-introduces-siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant/)), and shipped September 14, 2026 in beta, English only, with no availability on EU iPhones or in China ([Apple Newsroom, Sept 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/)). Third-party custom actions arrived as a developer preview in the iOS 27.3 betas on November 3: Siri "can only find custom content and perform custom actions an app makes explicitly available using App Intents", and the preview "isn't available to external testers in TestFlight or to versions of your app that you publish on the App Store" ([Apple Developer Documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/siri-ai-developer-preview)), with official rollout "scheduled... in the spring" ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/03/developers-can-now-create-advanced-app-skills-for-siri-ai/)). A hidden "Inference Providing" protocol in iOS 27 would let an outside provider replace Siri's server model and receive Siri's planner prompt, tool definitions and personal data, but **Apple "has not yet opened up the model delegation entitlement to third parties"**, and the "Ask..." menu is limited to ChatGPT ([MacRumors, Sept 14 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/14/siri-can-be-swapped-out-for-chatgpt-claude/)). There is **no system-wide MCP on iOS**: Apple's 2026 MCP support covers Xcode 27 plug-ins ([Apple Newsroom, June 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/apple-aids-app-development-with-new-intelligence-frameworks-and-advanced-tools/)) and Keynote, Pages and Numbers, where MCP "requires macOS 27 or later" ([Apple Newsroom, Oct 27 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-creator-studio-adds-powerful-new-ai-features-and-even-more-creative-tools/)).

iOS has no Android-style accessibility service, so other apps' UIs cannot be read or tapped. ReplayKit screen broadcasting is the only way to see another app's pixels; asked about background reading of other apps, Apple's developer support engineer called it "the only option for this sort of thing on iOS" and added, "I don't think you'll be able to make progress on this" ([Apple Developer Forums 818940](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/818940)). Developers report a memory cap of roughly 50 MB for broadcast extensions ([Apple Developer Forums 651367](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/651367)), and guideline 2.5.14 requires explicit consent and a visible indicator for any screen recording. XCUITest and WebDriverAgent can drive any app, but only as developer test tools launched from a Mac with Xcode ([appium-webdriveragent README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/appium-webdriveragent@15.0.0/README.md)). The App Review rules close the remaining doors: 2.5.1 and 2.5.2 limit apps to public APIs, forbid reading data outside the app's own container, and forbid executing downloaded code that changes functionality; **4.7.2 forbids exposing native platform APIs to plug-in software without Apple's permission**, a quiet risk for any design where connectors running inside the app touch HealthKit; and 5.1.2(i), revised in November 2025, requires explicit permission before sharing personal data "with third-party AI" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai)).

Apple's data hubs are useful but shallow. HealthKit grants read access per data type, yet apps write summaries: Hevy writes workout type, duration and energy while "set, rep, and weight detail stays in the Hevy app" ([Sahha](https://sahha.ai/integrations/hevy/)), MyFitnessPal writes one-way meal summaries with no individual foods ([Sonar Health](https://www.sonarhealth.co/en-AU/blog/sync-myfitnesspal-with-apple-health/)), and Cronometer exports about 13 nutrients ([Cronometer blog](https://cronometer.com/blog/apple-health)). EventKit gives full calendar and reminder access with permission, while Screen Time usage data cannot leave its sandboxed extension at all ([Apple Developer Forums 727958](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/727958)). The only force pushing iOS open is regulation. Apple said it "will not be able to ship Siri AI in the European Union" because regulators read the Digital Markets Act as requiring that any assistant get direct access to private data and control of installed apps; it proposed a "Trusted System Agent" phased in over 18 months, and the Commission "did not agree to any of Apple's proposals" ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/due-to-dma-siri-ai-delayed-in-eu-for-ios-27-and-ipados-27/); [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318136/20260610/eu-rejects-apple-siri-ai-exemption-commission-says-dma-never-blocked-launch.htm)). That fight is the most plausible future opening for third-party assistants on iPhone, but it is EU-only and has no timeline. Japan's rules produced something smaller: since iOS 26.2 users there can assign a third-party voice assistant to the side button, which changes the launch point and grants no new access ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/11/17/ios-26-2-iphone-users-japan-siri/)).

### Android: the universal route exists, and Google Play bans it for agents

Technically, Android allows the full "everything agent". An AccessibilityService can read the foreground app's UI tree, dispatch gestures and take screenshots ([Android reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/accessibilityservice/AccessibilityService)), and DroidRun used direct Accessibility API access with GPT-5 to complete **106 of 116 AndroidWorld tasks (91.4%)** in October 2025 ([Droidrun benchmark](https://droidrun.ai/benchmark/)). Policy forbids exactly that for Play apps. Since Google's October 30, 2025 update, "any use of this API that enables an app to autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions is prohibited", while "rule-based automation, where behavior follows a static, human-defined script" stays allowed ([Play announcement](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16550159?hl=en); [Play AccessibilityService policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491?hl=en)). The `isAccessibilityTool` flag is reserved for disability tools and explicitly excludes "automation tools, assistants", and everyone else needs an in-app disclosure, a Play Console declaration and a video of the consent flow. Sova AI, an accessibility-based LLM agent, was rejected by Play for "universal automation" and now ships as a self-hosted APK ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47613614)).

Sideloading is a shrinking escape hatch. Play Protect's "enhanced fraud protection" blocks sideloaded installs that request accessibility in 185 markets covering about 2.8 billion devices ([Bayton](https://bayton.org/android/android-enterprise-faq/enhanced-fraud-protection-enterprise/)); Android 17's opt-in Advanced Protection revokes accessibility from any app that is not a verified accessibility tool ([The Hacker News, Oct 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/android-17-advanced-protection-locks.html)); and apps can mark login, payment and financial views with `accessibilityDataSensitive` to hide them from non-tool services ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/12/enhancing-android-security-stop-malware.html?hl=en)). The reason is malware: Google calls accessibility "the main conduit" for malware and fraud, and in September 2026 the RatHat banking trojan began to use "the AI assistant to determine whether it needs to tap or scroll" ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/android-17-advanced-protection-locks.html); [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/09/new-android-malware-uses-ai-to-steal-bank-log)). At the API level a legitimate AI agent is indistinguishable from these trojans, or as one banking-security developer put it, "Android has no good way to tell malicious automation from legitimate automation" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47613614)).

Google's sanctioned routes are reserved for itself and its partners. **Computer Control**, which runs target apps in a secure virtual display that the assistant drives from screenshots, lets only "OEM-preloaded AI assistants" holding the privileged `ACCESS_COMPUTER_CONTROL` permission automate up to six target apps per session, and it is still a preview with "a limited set of target apps" ([Android Developers: Computer Control](https://developer.android.com/ai/computer-control)); Android 17 adds a per-assistant, per-app "Automation" permission and ends sessions after 60 minutes ([Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/18921033?hl=en)). **AppFunctions** lets apps "behave like on device MCP servers", but callers need `EXECUTE_APP_FUNCTIONS` and "only a limited number of apps and system agents can access the entire pipeline" ([Android Developers: AppFunctions](https://developer.android.com/ai/appfunctions)); it moved to beta at Android Dev Summit in late October 2026, alongside a Connected Apps Platform preview that links apps into Gemini "via Android MCP or cloud-hosted MCP servers" with 50+ partners ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-opens-android-development-to-third-party-ai-agents-and-unveils-new-gemini-apis/)). Gemini's own automation launched with eight U.S. apps (food delivery, rideshare, Starbucks, McDonald's) plus two Korean ones ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/25/gemini-can-now-automate-some-multi-step-tasks-on-android)); a November leak says 134 apps, but only on 2026 Galaxy flagships ([BestAITools](https://bestaitools.com/gemini-task-automation-is-coming-to-134-apps-but-only-on-2026-galaxy-flagships)). What a third party can legally use is MediaProjection screen capture, which needs consent for every session, shows a prominent status-bar chip, stops when the screen locks and can only read, not tap ([Android Developers: Media projection](https://developer.android.com/media/grow/media-projection)), plus Health Connect, which offers per-type permissions across seven categories with 30 days of history by default ([Health Connect data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/data-types)) and carries the same summary-level data as HealthKit.

Even where UI agents are allowed, they are slow and unreliable on real apps. AndroidWorld is saturated; on MobileWorld's longer, multi-app tasks the best framework scored **51.7%** ([arXiv 2512.19432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19432)), on AndroidDaily's 94 real closed-source apps Gemini 3 Flash topped out at **62.0% at 14.7 seconds per step** and the best GUI-specialist model reached 42.3% ([arXiv 2605.27761](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27761)), and an independent test of 65 real-world tasks found DroidRun best at 43% ([AIMultiple](https://research.aimultiple.com/mobile-ai-agent)). Target apps also fight back. When ByteDance shipped its Doubao assistant with system-level tap injection on a ZTE Nubia phone, WeChat force-logged-out its users and banks showed pop-ups telling users to disable it ([Yicai Global](https://www.yicaiglobal.com/news/wechat-some-chinese-banking-apps-reportedly-restrict-devices-with-bytedances-new-ai-voice-assistant)); by October 14, 2026, **155 apps**, 66 of them in Tencent's ecosystem, had formally refused GUI-agent operation under Doubao's own opt-out protocol ([PANews citing Caixin](https://panews.io/articles/01a13cf8-b32e-750e-8074-a39f759f3206)). The OEMs that avoided the fight went the structured route: Honor's YOYO runs on an MCP-based architecture that depends on app developers adapting their interfaces ([36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3896193801602697)).

| Route (Nov 2026, third-party store app) | iOS | Android |
|---|---|---|
| Read another app's UI or private data | Impossible: no API, 2.5.2 forbids it | AccessibilityService with disclosure; blind on sensitive views |
| Act autonomously in arbitrary apps | Impossible | Only if sideloaded; Play bans autonomous plan-and-act |
| OS agent framework | Siri AI App Toolbox (Siri only); model delegation closed (ChatGPT only) | Computer Control (OEM-preloaded only); AppFunctions calling gated |
| Call other apps' exposed actions | User-installed Shortcuts via x-callback-url; foreground, text result | AppFunctions in beta for limited callers; intents only launch apps |
| See the screen | ReplayKit broadcast with indicator; read-only | MediaProjection, consent each session; read-only |
| Health and fitness hub | HealthKit, summary level | Health Connect, summary level |
| System MCP | None (Xcode 27 and macOS iWork only) | AppFunctions described as on-device MCP; beta |
| Plausible future opening | EU DMA Article 6(7) fight; no timeline | AppFunctions and Computer Control broadening; no date for third parties |

## Per-app API engineering works from the cloud, not from the phone

The research ran nine target apps through every technique, with live probes on November 15, 2026: Canvas for students, the gym apps Strong, Hevy and Fitbod, Apple Fitness, and the nutrition apps MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, MacroFactor and Lose It. The consistent finding is that each technique reaches a different slice of apps, and the strongest ones run on a desktop or a server rather than on the user's phone.

### Official APIs reach two of nine targets, and both are gated

**Canvas** has a full REST API that students can reach with personal access tokens, and many community MCP servers build on it, including a PyPI package released in February 2026 ([PyPI canvas-mcp](https://pypi.org/project/canvas-mcp/1.1.0/)). Schools are closing that door: since September 2025 Canvas admins have a setting to stop students creating tokens ([Canvas Deploy Notes](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Releases/Canvas-Deploy-Notes-2025-09-10/ta-p/654577)), UW-Madison blocked student tokens from November 2025 ([UW-Madison KB](https://kb.wisc.edu/QueryLibrary/news.php?id=13955)), Ohio State re-enabled tokens for instructors in August 2026 and kept students barred ([Ohio State IT](https://it.osu.edu/news/2026/08/11/carmencanvas-personal-access-tokens)), and University of Missouri security argues the tokens bypass MFA ([UM System](https://tdx.umsystem.edu/TDClient/66/MOOnline/KB/ArticleDet?ID=2302)). **Hevy** publishes an OpenAPI 3.0 spec at version "0.0.1" with 22 operations, Pro-only, and warns "we make no guarantees that we won't completely change the structure or abandon the project entirely" ([Hevy Public API Docs](https://api.hevyapp.com/docs/)). The nutrition apps are closed: MyFitnessPal says "We are not accepting requests for API access at this time" ([myfitnesspalapi.com](https://myfitnesspalapi.com/)), Lose It runs a read-only partner beta where "Individuals are not able to be granted access" ([Lose It API](https://d2qjq4b9ehc3ag.cloudfront.net/api)), and Cronometer has no public API at all ([Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/3365/any-chance-a-public-api-for-cronometer-will-be-available)). An official API also does not mean AI use is allowed: Strava's 2024 agreement "explicitly prohibit[s] third parties from using any data obtained via Strava's API in artificial intelligence models" ([DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2024/11/stravas-changes-to-kill-off-apps.html)), and the Fitbit API is being replaced by a Google Health API whose scopes are all "Restricted" and need a privacy review ([Google Health API](https://developers.google.com/health/about?hl=it)).

### Spec-to-MCP generation is solved, spec quality is not

Stainless, Speakeasy and FastMCP all turn OpenAPI specs into MCP servers, with Stainless calling its generator experimental ([Speakeasy](https://www.speakeasy.com/docs/standalone-mcp); [Stainless](https://www.stainless.com/docs/guides/generate-an-mcp-server)). A live test shows where it breaks. **FastMCP rejected Hevy's real spec** with "Invalid OpenAPI schema" because of a non-standard `type: "enum"` on three schemas and a boolean `required` on a property; a 15-line normalizer fixed both, after which FastMCP produced 22 tools with truncated names like `Get_a_paginated_list_of_exercise_templates_available_on_`. Because the spec declares no `securitySchemes`, the `api-key` header surfaced as an argument on every tool, meaning the model would be asked to supply the secret itself ([Hevy spec source](https://api.hevyapp.com/docs/)). **Canvas publishes legacy Swagger 1.2 with 1,139 operations** across 148 resource files ([Canvas api-docs.json](https://canvas.instructure.com/doc/api/api-docs.json)), far too many to expose as tools, which is why working community servers already trim it to role-specific subsets. For an "engineer its own API" system, generation is a pipeline step rather than a route: repair the spec, cut the tool list to the 15 to 30 operations a student or lifter actually needs, and lift authentication out of tool arguments into the transport.

### Traffic reverse engineering already ships working write tools

Automated tooling exists. Integuru records a HAR (the browser's log of its network requests) plus cookies while you perform an action, traces each dynamic parameter back to the request that produced it until only auth cookies remain, and emits runnable Python ([Integuru](https://github.com/Integuru-AI/Integuru)); reverse-api-engineer turns Playwright traffic into a typed client ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/reverse-api-engineer/)); and Unbrowse learns a site's internal "shadow API" routes from real browsing, pools them in a route graph shared across users, and executes cached routes in about **950 ms versus 3,404 ms** for Playwright across 94 domains ([arXiv 2604.00694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00694)). The proof sits in the nutrition apps that refuse APIs. `mfp-mcp` explains that MyFitnessPal "moved behind Cloudflare + NextAuth, which broke the username/password login that most existing integrations rely on", so it reuses the browser session cookie, impersonates Chrome's TLS fingerprint, and can log, replace and delete diary entries, shipping 12 releases between July and November 2026 ([PyPI mfp-mcp](https://pypi.org/project/mfp-mcp/)). `cronometer-api-mcp` reverse-engineered Cronometer's Android API through static analysis of the compiled `libapp.so`, traffic interception with Frida and mitmproxy, and trial and error; it adds and removes food entries, handles 2FA codes, and shipped **20 releases in six months**, which signals both active upkeep and frequent breakage ([PyPI cronometer-api-mcp](https://pypi.org/project/cronometer-api-mcp)). MyFitnessPal's history (a hidden login captcha in 2022, then the Cloudflare and NextAuth move, then a client-side food form that broke writes) shows breakage arriving roughly yearly, so re-capture on failure is mandatory ([python-myfitnesspal docs](https://python-myfitnesspal.readthedocs.io/en/latest/getting_started.html)).

This is where "through the phone" breaks. Web-backed apps need only one browser session to capture traffic. Mobile-only apps (Strong, Fitbod, MacroFactor) need APK analysis and Frida on a rooted or emulated Android device attached to a desktop, and when an app enforces Google's strict Play Integrity device verdict, every spoofing tool in a March 2026 SANS test failed ([SANS](https://www.sans.org/white-papers/can-root-detection-be-trusted-bypass-techniques-mobile-platforms)). On iPhone, Proxyman proves on-device capture is possible through a local VPN and a root certificate the user generates and trusts ([Proxyman](https://apps.apple.com/app/proxyman/id1551292695)), but that only works for apps that do not pin certificates, and asking consumers to trust a root certificate is a security red flag. Execution location matters too: Fitbod's site returned a Cloudflare challenge (403) to a plain request, and `mfp-mcp` notes that datacenter IPs get challenged more often ([fitbod.me](https://www.fitbod.me/); [PyPI mfp-mcp](https://pypi.org/project/mfp-mcp/)), which favors executing from the user's device or a residential connection.

### Browser agents are the universal fallback, at a steep premium

A logged-in browser agent is the most general route for any app with a web version. On Browser Use's 180-task live-site benchmark, its own agent scores 72.9 at **11 cents per task** and the top model run reaches 79.6 ([Browser Use benchmarks](https://browser-use.com/benchmarks)), against sub-second, effectively free calls through a learned API. Browserbase "contexts" persist cookies so a user logs in once ([Browserbase docs](https://docs.browserbase.com/guides/authentication)), and Stagehand caches successful actions and replays them without model calls, falling back to inference only when a selector breaks ([Stagehand docs](https://docs.stagehand.dev/best-practices/caching)). The economical design uses the browser agent for discovery and repair, then compiles what it learns into direct API calls. Schools that front Canvas with SSO and Duo need a human for the first login, and the route is closed entirely for apps with no web version: MacroFactor's `app.` subdomain is just an app-store link page ([app.macrofactor.com](https://app.macrofactor.com/)).

### UI automation, Shortcuts and exports fill the gaps shallowly

Recent research converts one recorded run into a reusable tool. GOAL replays a demonstrated workflow while a "sniffer" agent captures the API calls underneath and emits a parameterized skill ([TDCommons GOAL](https://www.tdcommons.org/dpubs_series/10260)), and Salesforce's GPA records one demonstration into a template exposed as an MCP tool ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/gpa-gui-process-automation/)). That is the only universal route for mobile-only apps, it works only on Android, and the 43% to 62% real-world success rates above make it too unreliable for unattended writes like logging a meal. On iOS the write path is Shortcuts when an app exposes actions; a community MacroFactor MCP writes food logs through MacroFactor's Shortcuts action ([Glama macrofactor-mcp](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/NasserAlbusaidi/macrofactor-mcp)). For history, exports are the lowest-risk read: Strong and Fitbod export CSV, Lose It exports three CSVs from its website, and MacroFactor has an in-app export ([strong.app](https://strong.app/); [fitdata](https://github.com/rrebase/fitdata)). GDPR Article 20 guarantees portability "in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format" ([GDPR Art. 20](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-20-gdpr/)), but formal requests are far too slow for an interactive product, and export-ingest data is stale by design.

| App | Read today | Write today | Best route |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canvas (student) | Easy if the school allows tokens, hard if not | Moderate with tokens | Official REST curated to ~20 tools; persisted browser session as fallback |
| Hevy | Easy | Easy (no delete endpoints) | Official API, Pro only |
| MyFitnessPal | Moderate | Moderate (diary log, replace, delete) | Community MCP on session cookie plus TLS impersonation |
| Cronometer | Moderate | Moderate (add and remove food) | Community MCP on reverse-engineered mobile API |
| Lose It | Moderate via web CSV | Hard | Export ingest; unofficial client |
| MacroFactor | Moderate via manual export | Hard; iPhone Shortcuts only | Export plus Shortcuts |
| Strong, Fitbod | Moderate via CSV and health-store summaries | Very hard | Export ingest; Android UI automation |
| Apple Fitness and Health | Easy on device | Possible on device, impossible from a server | Native companion app |

None of these ratings was validated with logged-in accounts, and no community MCP server publishes write-reliability numbers. The ratings still settle the framing question. "On the spot" generation is the wrong frame: discovering an app's API is a one-time cost that can be shared across every user (the point of Unbrowse's shared route graph), while credentials and sessions are personal and stay with the user. The product compiles each app once, centrally, and keeps recompiling it as the app changes.

## The Ninth Circuit made architecture a legal question

The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA, the U.S. anti-hacking law) stopped covering mere terms-of-service violations after Van Buren and the hiQ litigation, though hiQ still lost on breach of contract ([Fenwick](https://fenwick.com/insights/publications/hiq-labs-scrapes-by-again-the-ninth-circuit-reaffirms-that-data-scraping-does-not-violate-the-cfaa-1)). In March 2026 a district court enjoined Perplexity's Comet browser agent from Amazon, reasoning that a user's permission is not Amazon's authorization ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-10/amazon-wins-court-order-blocking-perplexity-s-ai-shopping-bots)). **On August 4, 2026 the Ninth Circuit vacated that order**: "However advanced the Assistant currently is, it is a tool, not a person for statutory purposes", so the user, not Perplexity, accessed Amazon. The court leaned on the fact that Comet captures screenshots locally and "Perplexity itself does not directly communicate with Amazon's servers", distinguishing *Power Ventures*, where the defendant's own systems contacted Facebook ([Ninth Circuit opinion](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/amazon-v-perplexity-ninth-circuit-opinion.pdf)). The limits are real. The panel wrote "We do not establish a new legal regime governing agentic AI", a footnote preserves Amazon's ability to regulate access "via private terms of service", the record was preliminary, and Amazon was weighing further review ([Eric Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2026/08/ninth-circuit-lifts-restrictions-on-agentic-ai-accessing-amazon.htm)). Cooley's reading is that agents connecting server to server, without the user's machine as relay, carry more risk ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-06-ninth-circuit-rules-on-ai-agent-access-to-third-party-websites-under-cfaa)).

That turns architecture into a legal variable. In *Power Ventures*, once Facebook sent a cease-and-desist, "permission from the users alone was not sufficient" ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/13-17102/13-17102-2016-12-09.html)). A hosted MCP server that stores a user's MyFitnessPal cookie and calls MFP from the vendor's datacenter looks like *Power Ventures*; an app that runs the same requests from the user's phone or a browser the user controls looks like Perplexity. This creates a genuine design tension, because the legally safer execution location (the device) is also the one iOS restricts most, with guideline 2.5.4 limiting background execution to sanctioned modes ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Device-executed calls therefore happen mostly while the user has the app open, and scheduled jobs fall back to the riskier server posture.

Contract and copyright risk are more concrete than CFAA risk. Cronometer's terms terminate the license on any attempt to "circumvent any technical protection measures" and bar scraping to collect content for AI training ([Cronometer Terms](https://cronometer.com/terms)); MyFitnessPal grants only a "limited, revocable, personal, non-transferable, and non-exclusive" license ([MyFitnessPal Terms](https://www.myfitnesspal.com/terms-of-service)). DMCA section 1201 exemptions can cover the act of circumvention but never trafficking in the tools ([Software Freedom Conservancy](https://sfconservancy.org/blog/2020/dec/16/dmca-exemptions-explained/)), so shipping certificate-pinning or attestation bypasses to users is the single riskiest move available. Canvas writes add school acceptable-use policies and academic-integrity exposure, and sending HealthKit data to an LLM has to clear Apple's 5.1.2(i) consent rule and stay within 5.1.3's "improving health management" purpose ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). In practice the risk is blocking, not prosecution. In October 2026 Amazon blocked Meta's Muse shopping agent, Yelp allowed only agents that pay for data licensing, Delta blocked agent bookings, and JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab confirmed they block agents from accounts ([The Verge via Slashdot](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/26/10/18/1715218/); [MIT Technology Review](https://technologyreview.com/2026/10/19/1147186/making-the-internet-better-for-ai-agents-might-make-it-worse-for-everyone-else)). For a user the realistic downside is a suspended account; for a vendor at scale it is cease-and-desist letters that convert future access into CFAA exposure.

## Every giant connects an allowlist, and nobody owns the long tail

Every platform owner shipped "AI across your apps" in 2026, each behind a wall. Gemini's screen automation started with eight U.S. apps ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/25/gemini-can-now-automate-some-multi-step-tasks-on-android)). Claude added **15 consumer connectors** (AllTrails, Instacart, Uber, Credit Karma and others) in April 2026 from a directory of over 200, with mobile in beta ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/connectors-for-everyday-life)), plus Apple Health and Health Connect in a U.S.-only beta ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/01/22/claude-ai-adds-apple-health-connectivity/)). ChatGPT Health connects Apple Health and MyFitnessPal ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/01/07/apple-health-integration-launches-in-new-chatgpt-health-feature)), and at DevDay on September 29 OpenAI launched **Dots**, always-on agents with their own cloud computer and browser that connect to 4,000+ apps, included only on the Pro and Business Premium plans, against a ChatGPT base of 1.2 billion weekly users ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/379584/openai-ai-agents-computers-devday-2026-everything-announced)). Microsoft's Copilot connects to Outlook, Gmail and Google Calendar on mobile ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/microsoft-copilot/connecting-microsoft-copilot-to-other-services)). Two mechanisms result: OS-level control on curated allowlists, which only the OS owner can do, and cloud connector directories, which work across platforms but reach only apps with a public API and a listing.

Consumer agent startups proved engagement, not margins. Poke reached hundreds of thousands of users and 100 million messages in three months over iMessage, SMS, Telegram and WhatsApp, then sold to Cognition at a "low nine figures" valuation; its co-founder said it "was expensive to run, which made it hard to turn a profit" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/why-cognition-bought-poke-ai-personality-is-becoming-a-competitive-advantage/)). OpenClaw, the self-hosted open-source agent, became the real consumer phenomenon until Gartner labeled it an "unacceptable cybersecurity risk" ([SecurityWeek](https://securityweek.com/openclaw-security-issues-continue-as-secureclaw-open-source-tool-debuts)) and its creator joined OpenAI ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4132725/openai-hires-openclaw-founder-as-ai-agent-race-intensifies.html)). Most of the money went to the integration layer sold to developers and enterprises: Arcade's $60 million Series A in June 2026 ([Arcade](https://www.arcade.dev/blog/arcade-series-a/)), Composio's $25 million ([Composio](https://composio.dev/content/series-a)), Workday's deal for Pipedream and its ~3,000 integrations ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/11/19/workday-acquire-pipedream-extend-ai-agent-integrations-across-enterprise-apps/)), and Zapier MCP, which charges two tasks per tool call ([Zapier docs](https://docs.zapier.com/mcp/usage.md)). Mobile-control startups found their paying customers in app testing: Minitap raised a seed for agents that operate virtual phones for development and testing ([StartupMag](https://www.startupmag.co.uk/funding/minitap-2025-seed-funding)), and DroidRun aimed its funding at an enterprise cloud platform ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2025/07/23/droidrun-raises-eur21m-pre-seed-to-scale-mobile-native-ai-agent-infrastructure/)). Hardware failed outright (HP bought Humane's remains for $116 million ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/humane-ends-its-ai-pin-in-ten-days-sells-technology-to-hp-for-116-million/))), and Rabbit relaunched as OS3, a cloud agent reached over iMessage and Telegram that operates the user's computers, not their phone apps ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/?p=385383)). The closest funded bet to a "personal MCP" is Hello Haven's $15 million pre-seed, on the thesis that the durable consumer layer is the user's memory and permissions above whichever model does each task ([Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/frank-addante-hello-haven-15m-personal-ai-digital-twin)).

Demand points at the same gap. In Menlo Ventures' July 2026 survey of 5,067 U.S. adults, agent users connect email (36%), browsers (33%), messaging (31%), cloud storage (29%), calendars (27%), **health apps (23%)** and financial accounts (20%); the average AI user runs **3.0 assistants**; AI already reaches **56% of people doing school or work assignments**; and 76% of holdouts cite privacy ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)). Only 10% of people in EY's 23-market study had let an agent buy anything ([CXM World](https://cxm.world/?p=141939)), which puts trust, not capability, as the binding constraint. The clearest signal comes from power users: community MCP servers already exist for Canvas, Hevy, MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, Lose It and MacroFactor, several shipping releases monthly, which is demand the platforms' allowlists are not serving.

## The buildable version is a connector compiler with a phone-shaped front end

The version that can ship in November 2026 has two halves. The cloud half is a hosted connector compiler that climbs a fixed ladder per app: first an official API or OAuth where AI use is permitted, then a maintained community MCP server judged by release recency, then a one-time traffic capture on the app's web version compiled into a client (spec normalized to OpenAPI 3.1, cut to 15 to 30 tools, auth injected at the transport, re-captured on failure), then a browser agent on the web app as runtime fallback and repair loop that compiles back to API calls in the Stagehand and Unbrowse pattern, then Android UI automation or iPhone Shortcuts for mobile-only apps, and finally export ingest for history. Discovery happens once per app and is shared; credentials stay per user. The surface the agent sees should be Executor's: a fixed tool set with code mode, accounts separate from connectors, and approval functions that read each call's input. Because Executor is MIT-licensed and already ships the vault, approvals, multi-account support and versioned apps, a builder can run it as the backbone and add only the derivation layer it lacks, along with the egress allowlist and deploy approval that AI-generated connectors need before they touch real tokens.

The phone half is a native companion app on both platforms that does what only the phone can. It reads (and where allowed writes) HealthKit, Health Connect and EventKit through its own native code, never through plug-in code, to stay clear of guideline 4.7.2. It hosts the in-app browser where users sign into web versions of their apps, so sessions are captured on the device. On iPhone it offers a gallery of shortcuts for apps that expose App Intents, it accepts shared content and screenshots for apps nothing else reaches, and it turns approval functions into push notifications ("log meals freely, ask before submitting coursework"). It should also publish its own App Intents and AppFunctions so that Siri AI and Gemini can call it, since being the target those assistants call is allowed and calling into other apps is not. On Android an optional power tier can follow OpenAlly's split, where the Play app avoids autonomous accessibility use and a separate sideloaded companion handles screen automation ([OpenAlly](https://openally.ai/blog/how-ai-agents-do-things-on-your-phone)), with every Play Protect and Advanced Protection caveat above attached.

The economics work because the product is an MCP server, not an agent. Like Executor, it "runs no AI itself", so the main path carries none of the inference cost that sank Poke's margins; that cost sits in the user's existing ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini plan. What remains is discovery and browser fallback (cents to dollars per run, amortized across every user of that app) and maintenance on the scale of Cronometer's 20 releases in six months, repeated for every app. The honest promise is not "any app" but "any app with a cloud API, a web version, a health-store footprint or Shortcuts actions"; writes to Strong and Fitbod on iPhone stay out of reach.

## Conclusion

The phone's role flips from runtime to key and witness: its job is to hold sessions, produce the data that only exists on the device, and press approve, while the engineering happens in a shared cloud that compiles each app once and recompiles it as the app breaks. That reframes the moat. Connectors are rentable from Composio, Arcade, Zapier and the open-source community, and models are commodities, so what can be defended is a self-repairing route graph for the long tail of consumer apps, maintained faster than those apps change, plus the user's permission graph across assistants. It also points to a vertical rather than horizontal wedge: students and fitness or nutrition logging with writes, where demand is high (56% AI penetration on assignments, 23% of agent users connecting health apps), official connectors are weakest, and the platforms' health integrations read only the shallow summaries that HealthKit and Health Connect carry. Sets, reps, food line items and coursework are exactly what the allowlists leave out.

Three clocks decide how long the opening lasts. If Apple's custom Siri AI actions reach the App Store in the spring (most likely 2027) and Apple opens model delegation beyond ChatGPT, iPhone access belongs to whoever Siri hosts. If AppFunctions calling opens to third-party apps, Android becomes the first platform where a neutral layer can invoke other apps on the device with the OS's blessing, and a builder should be ready on day one. If OpenAI moves Dots down from its top tiers to Plus, a 4,000-app agent lands in front of a billion users. The bet is not that the platforms will fail to build cross-app agents, because they already have. The bet is that each will only ever connect its own assistant to its own allowlist, and that users running three assistants will want one layer that connects all of them to everything else.
